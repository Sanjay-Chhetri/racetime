One event can hold several distances — a 5K and a 10K on the same morning. Each race is ranked on its own, so nobody is placed against a field that ran a different distance. Leave the start time blank to use the event gun; set it for a staggered start.

Sequence orders the columns in results. Each race needs exactly one finish. Checkpoint names must be unique across the whole event, so name them 5K Finish and 10K Finish rather than both Finish.

Race artwork

A wide banner image works best — it runs across the top of every bib and fills the finisher certificate. PNG, JPEG or WebP. A photo straight off a phone is fine: anything too large to upload is resized here in the browser before it is sent.

Full artwork looks best and is what runners keep. The band uses far less ink and stays readable on cheap paper — print one of each before you commit to a few hundred.

Race photo

A photograph of the race — runners on the road, the start line, the view. It leads the race on the home page and runs across the top of the results, which are the two screens somebody reaches knowing nothing about your race. Landscape works best.

This is the only one of the three that is a photograph. The race artwork above is the banner printed on every bib; the certificate background below is the portrait card a finisher keeps.

Finisher certificate

The bib is a wide landscape card and the certificate is tall (4:5, like an Instagram post). One image cannot suit both — whichever way it is composed, the other crop is wrong. Upload a portrait image here, around 1080 × 1350 px. Leave it empty and the bib artwork is used, as before.

How people pay you

RaceTime never touches the money and holds no bank or card details. People pay you directly by UPI and type in the reference; you check it against your own account and tick it off.
