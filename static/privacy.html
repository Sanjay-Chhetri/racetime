What is kept

If you enter a race or open an account, RaceTime stores your name, the email address and phone number you give, the town you say you are from, the year you were born, and an emergency contact if you supply one for a race.

It also stores every sighting of your bib at a checkpoint, and the results worked out from them.

What is public

Race results are public. Your name, bib number, category, finishing time and position appear on the results page for anyone to see, the same as they would on a printed sheet pinned up after a race. That is what a result is.

Your profile is private until you change it. Your photo, the town you are from, what you have written about yourself and all your races gathered in one place are visible only to you and the organisers, unless you choose otherwise in My running → Profile. You can change your mind at any time.

Your email address, phone number and emergency contact are never shown publicly, whatever your profile setting. They are visible to race organisers, who need them to run the race and to reach you if something goes wrong.

Under 18

An account for someone under 18 cannot have a public or members-only profile. The setting is refused by the server, not just hidden. Their results still appear on the results page, because a race result is a race result — but their photo, town and history are not gathered into a page anyone can browse.

If a child is entered by a parent or a school, the organiser holds those details on the entry rather than the child holding an account.

Visitor counting

Visits to the site are counted without a cookie and without storing any address. Each visit is reduced to a one-way hash that changes every midnight, so people can be counted for a day and nobody can be followed across days or identified. Nothing in that table can be turned back into a person.

Removing your details

Write to the organiser through the form on the home page and your account and profile can be removed. Results cannot be un-published — a race that happened, happened, and the results of a public race stay the record of it, the same as a printed sheet.
